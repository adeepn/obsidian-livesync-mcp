Defects found by this run
-------------------------
1. Stale PREFERRED after a settings change (production). The client keeps a changed
   recommendation-only setting in its own map and does not republish PREFERRED:
   B-reconnect-before-fix.json shows the device map at 100 and PREFERRED at 50, and the
   client had uploaded a 100 MiB file. The peer of 3d812ff resolved PREFERRED first and
   treated exact.bin as oversized. Fixed by 37f63b0 "fix: derive recommendation-only policy
   from devices". Confirmed in this one-device vault in both directions: B-size PASS after
   the fix, B50 PASS after lowering the limit.
2. Check helper stopped on a client-ingested file (helper only). reconnect refused
   invalid.md, which the client had stored with invalid bytes replaced (controller: no
   transcript kept). Fixed by caf8665 "fix: compare reconnect files with device-ingested
   bodies".
3. Checklist step 0 promised the wrong thing (documentation). It said an ordinary sync after
   a confirmed wipe would re-send the files. Observation 0 shows that it does not. The step
   was corrected earlier on this branch, in 5cfe58a "docs: correct device rebuild after a
   confirmed remote wipe".

Limits of this record
---------------------
- Scope of the file comparison. reconnect compares the files that the size policy admits
  and the peer's scope covers. Hidden files (11) are outside the scope; above.bin, and
  exact.bin at limit 50, are above the policy. invalid.md is compared with the text the
  client stored, not with the 15 bytes on disk. "PASS" therefore means: every compared file
  equals the client's stored form, byte for byte, with equal type, metadata size and
  piece ids.
- The large files are a repeating 13-byte pattern (local_prepare in the helper). The client
  cut every piece at the maximum size, 102,400 bytes, with a last piece of 61,440 bytes for
  device-40m.bin, and stored identical pieces once: journal-inventory.txt shows 14 stored
  pieces for the 1,434 piece references of the two files, and the same 14 piece sizes in the
  peer's journal for its own 40 MiB file. The steps prove piece counts, ids, sizes, storage
  once and end-to-end transfer of large files. They do not prove cut positions on varied
  bytes against a real client. Cut positions are covered by the offline oracle differentials
  against the pinned upstream code.
- The lossless legacy cut (fea689f) has no live evidence: the vault uses V3.
- Obsidian's file explorer hides .bin files by default. The owner could not see them and
  asked the controller to check; disk-listing.txt is that check.
- What remains of the test vault: file names, sizes, SHA-256 digests and short revision
  prefixes, and the names of the six notes that were in the vault before this run. No text
  of those six notes.
