Defects found by this run
-------------------------
1. Stale PREFERRED after a settings change (production). The client keeps a changed
   recommendation-only setting in its own map and does not republish PREFERRED: the
   device map held 100 while PREFERRED held 50, and the client uploaded a 100 MiB file.
   The peer resolved PREFERRED first and treated exact.bin as oversized. Fixed by
   37f63b0 "fix: derive recommendation-only policy from devices" (device maps first,
   PREFERRED only when no device advertises the key). Confirmed live in both
   directions: B-size PASS after the fix, B50 PASS after lowering the limit.
2. Check helper stopped on a client-ingested file (helper only). reconnect refused
   invalid.md, which the client had stored with invalid bytes replaced. Fixed by
   caf8665 "fix: compare reconnect files with device-ingested bodies".
3. Checklist step 0 was wrong (documentation). It prescribed an ordinary sync after a
   confirmed wipe; the observation above shows that sync does not re-send files.

Limits of this record
---------------------
- The large files are a repeating 14-byte pattern (local_prepare in the helper). The
  client cut every piece at the maximum size, 102,400 bytes, with a last piece of
  61,440 bytes for device-40m.bin, and stored identical pieces once: the client's
  journal for both files holds 14 chunk records for 1,434 piece references. The steps
  prove piece counts, ids, sizes, deduplication and end-to-end transfer of large
  files. They do not prove cut positions on varied bytes against a real client.
  Cut positions are covered by the offline oracle differentials against the pinned
  upstream code.
- The lossless legacy cut (fea689f) has no live evidence: the vault uses V3.
- Obsidian's file explorer hides .bin files by default. The owner could not see them;
  the controller checked those names and bytes on disk.
